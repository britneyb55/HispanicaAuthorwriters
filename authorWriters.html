Men Behind the Stories

Javier Zamora
Early Life and Background
  • Born on April 6, 1990, in La Herradura, El Salvador.
  • Raised by his grandparents after his parents left El Salvador.
  • Moved to the United States in 1999, at age nine, to reunite with his parents.
  • Grew up in San Rafael, California.
Education
  • Attended The Branson School in Marin County, California.
  • Earned a B.A. in History with a minor in Creative Writing from UC Berkeley (2008–2012).
  • Earned an M.F.A. in Creative Writing from New York University (2012–2014).

Featured Book

Solito: A Memoir (2022)

In this memoir, Zamora recounts his journey from El Salvador to the United States as a child and his attempt to reunite with his parents.

Key Themes
  • Migration and family separation
  • Identity and belonging
  • Childhood and resilience
A Passage to Remember

"I ask them if they will miss me. 'Absolutely,' they say, because no one who's left to La USA has ever come back to visit."

— Javier Zamora, Solito, p. 9

Richard Rodriguez
Early Life and Background
  • Born in San Francisco, California, to Mexican immigrant parents.
  • Grew up in Sacramento, California.
  • At age six, Richard Rodriguez began attending Sacred Heart School in Sacramento, a school run by the Sisters of Mercy. This marked an important change in his life as he began learning and using English more extensively outside his Spanish-speaking home.
Education
  • Bacholer of Art (B.A. ) in English, Stanford University in 1967.
  • Master of Arts (M.A.) in Philosophy, Columbia University in 1969.
  • Pursued graduate studies in English Renaissance literature at the University of California, Berkeley but left academia.

Featured Book

Hunger of Memory: The Education of Richard Rodriguez (1982)

In this memoir, Rodriguez reflects on growing up in a Mexican American family and learning English in school. He describes how education helped him succeed while also changing his relationship with his parents and the Spanish language.

Key Themes
  • Language and cultural identity
  • Family and belonging
  • Education and social mobility
  • The relationship between personal identity and assimilation
A Passage to Remember

"One Saturday morning I entered the kitchen where my parents were talking, but I did not realize that they were talking in Spanish until, the moment they saw me, their voices changed and they began speaking English [...] I felt my throat twisted by unsounded grief."

— Richard Rodriguez, Hunger of Memory, pp. 576–577

Women Behind the Stories

Reyna Grande
Early Life and Background
  • Born on September 7, 1975, in Iguala, Guerrero, Mexico.
  • Her father left for the United States when she was two years old. Two years later, her mother followed, leaving Reyna and her siblings in Mexico.
  • She and her siblings were raised by their paternal grandparents in conditions of poverty. At age nine, Reyna moved to Los Angeles, California, with her siblings after her father brought them to the United States.
Education
  • Attended Aldama Elementary School in Highland Park, California, after arriving in the United States without knowing English.
  • Graduated from Benjamin Franklin High School in 1993.
  • Attended Pasadena City College before transferring to the University of California, Santa Cruz, where she earned a Bachelor of Arts (B.A.) in Creative Writing and Film & Video in 1999.
  • Earned a Master of Fine Arts (M.F.A.) in Creative Writing from Antioch University.

Featured Book

The Distance Between Us (2012)

In this memoir, Reyna Grande shares her childhood experiences growing up in Mexico while separated from her parents. She describes being raised by her paternal grandparents, the challenges of immigrating to the United States, and adjusting to a new country and learning English.

Key Themes
  • Family separation and immigration
  • Childhood hardship and resilience
  • Identity and belonging
  • Language and cultural adjustment
  • Education and the pursuit of a better future
A Passage to Remember

"I cried for my hair. It was the only beautiful thing I had."

— Reyna Grande, The Distance Between Us, p. 28

Erika L. Sánchez
Early Life and Background
  • Born in 1984 and raised in Cicero, Illinois, near Chicago.
  • Her parents immigrated from Mexico, influencing her connection to Mexican culture and family traditions.
  • She developed a love for writing during childhood and dreamed of becoming an author.
Education
  • Earned her bachelor's degree from the University of Illinois Chicago.
  • Received a Fulbright scholarship to teach English in Madrid, Spain.
  • Earned her M.F.A. in Creative Writing from the University of New Mexico.

Featured Book

I Am Not Your Perfect Mexican Daughter (2017)

Julia struggles with grief and her parents' expectations after her older sister, Olga, dies in an accident. As she explores Olga's belongings, she uncovers secrets about her sister's life. Along the way, Julia faces her own struggles and works toward her dream of attending college in New York.

Key Themes
  • Cultural identity and belonging
  • Family expectations and traditions
  • Grief and loss
  • Independence and ambition
A Passage to Remember

“I want Amá to tell me that she loves me and that we'll get through this together, but she doesn't. ... I stand there like a dope, waiting and waiting for her to say something that will make me feel better.”

— Erika L. Sánchez, I Am Not Your Perfect Mexican Daughter, p. 15
